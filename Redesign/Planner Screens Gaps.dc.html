Batch 48 · the profile drawer

The person, not the planner

Asked for and missing: a pop-out for who you are and how this looks. It is a drawer, not a window, because it opens from a person — your own avatar, or a row in People & roles — and a drawer is how this app opens records. Four tabs, and the Display tab is deliberately the same six controls as the settings pane rather than a second set.

A person is a record

Which is why this is a 360px drawer with a tab strip and a two-button footer, exactly like a guest or a vendor. The settings window is about the planner; this is about a person in it.

One store, two doors

Density, currency and motion appear here and in Settings → Display & density. Same values, two entry points — density is changed hourly and should not cost four clicks.

Opened on someone else, it shows less

The same four tabs on a helper hide money and show a shorter Access list. On a vendor it becomes the portal scope contract. One drawer, three depths.

Alerts belong to the person

Two planners on one file want different interruptions. What the app generates is a settings pane; what reaches you is on your profile — and nobody sees anyone else’s unread count.

Batch 47 · settings, every pane

Fourteen panes and one pointer

The previous batch drew three panes and the window they live in. This one draws all of them, so there is no pane Cursor has to invent — including a fourth Help group holding Get started, the page-by-page guide and the FAQ, and the Wedding setup entry, which stays a pointer to its page rather than a pane.

The device / planner / file split

The test is what a restore does to the setting. Device settings die with the browser. Planner settings travel in the backup. File settings are the backup. A setting that fits none of the three is content in disguise — which is exactly what Wedding setup turned out to be.

Four panes are pointers, not preferences

Day-of mode arms a mode drawn elsewhere. Notifications decides what reaches a panel drawn elsewhere. Import opens a mapper drawn elsewhere. Documents sets what the Print Centre assembles with. Each says so, with the batch number.

Some controls are shown locked

Vendor scope and “a contract total is authoritative” are drawn as disabled toggles rather than omitted. A visible limit is understood; a missing one becomes a support question.

One pane has no undo

Emptying trash. It is the only action in the app that cannot be reversed, and it is the only place that says so in those words.

FAQ
Twelve questions, and they are the ones actually asked — three about numbers that will not change, four about where the file lives, and five about what the app refuses to do. Answers state the reason, not the workaround.
All 12NumbersThe fileLimitsPrinting
▸
Why can’t I type this number?
It is derived. Confirmed guests, category totals and outstanding balances are calculated from the records beneath them — typing one would make two figures disagree. Edit the record it comes from; the drawer names it.
▸
Where is my planner stored?
In one file on this device, in plain SQLite, with a localStorage mirror for crash safety. No account, no cloud, nothing uploaded. That is why Backup is the only way to move to another machine.
▸
Why did changing one date move four things?
Tasks carry dependencies. The confirmation before a date change lists exactly what moves — it is not a warning, it is the receipt.
▸
Can the couple see everything I see?
Almost. They own Covenant and grant you access to it; you own nothing they cannot see. Helpers see no money by default, and vendors see only their own scope, which cannot be turned off.
▸
Why won’t it email my guests?
It has no mail account and no server. It composes the message and copies it out to yours, so the send comes from you and lands in your sent folder.
▸
What happens if I lose this laptop?
You lose everything since the last backup. There is no recovery, because there is nowhere for the app to recover from. Backup weekly is not advice, it is the design.

Batch 46 · the record editor window

The same record, with room

Every drawer footer offers Full editor, and until now only the guest editor was drawn. The window is not a different form: same tabs, same order, same fields, laid out in three columns instead of one. Three record types are drawn — Task, Vendor and Payment — which is enough to fix the pattern for the other twenty-seven.

Tabs become a left rail, not a wider strip

The drawer’s four tabs are a horizontal strip because 360px allows nothing else. At 1240px they become a vertical list, which reads faster and leaves the tab set open to a fifth entry without shrinking type.

The third column is the consequence column

Blockers, documents outstanding, what a payment leaves behind. It is the drawer’s warning boxes given a permanent home rather than being interleaved with fields.

Prev / next carries the list

The window inherits the filter and sort of the page it was opened from — “12 of 84” means twelfth of the current view, not of all tasks. Working a filtered list one record at a time is the whole reason the window exists.

Derived fields are stated, not hidden

The vendor’s money block is drawn as ordinary fields with a heading that says read-only. Hiding derived values makes the planner hunt; disabling them without a reason makes it look broken.

Batch 45 · the top bar, opened

Four menus that were only ever referenced

The top bar is drawn on all 51 base screens, but every one of them draws it closed. Six build notes across the other files route the reader to “the avatar menu” for viewer preferences, trash, day-of mode and the shortcut sheet — a menu nobody had drawn. Here are all four of its open states.

There is no gear icon

Settings hangs off the avatar already in the bar. A gear beside it would put two settings affordances within 30px of each other, and the planner would learn neither.

Batch 44 · the four drawers that were missing

Guest, task, payment, vendor

The drawer audit covered thirty record types and skipped the four with the most traffic — the ones a planner opens fifty times a day. Eighteen tabs, drawn to the same anatomy as Planner Screens Drawers: header with record type and status chips, tab strip, body, two-button footer, and a History tab that separates typed changes from derived ones.

A drawer tabs by the questions asked of it

Not details / more details. A guest is asked who she is, which envelope she belongs to, where she sits, and what she replied — so those are the tabs, and each one has an owner elsewhere in the app.

Inherited fields say where they come from

A guest’s address lives on the household. Rather than duplicating it into the guest, the tab states the inheritance and sends you to the owner — one address, one edit, one envelope.

Every drawer names its consequence

Pay this and Venue hits 91%. Move this date and three tasks move. Shorten this turnaround and two vendors are at risk. The number that matters is rarely the one on the record.

History separates typed from derived

Derived lines are indented with an arrow, greyed, and never editable. They also do not count toward “last touched”, so a record does not look worked-on because something upstream moved.
