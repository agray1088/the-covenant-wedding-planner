The last thirty-eight tabs

Note, packet, template, printable, hub table, hub row, essentials item, name-change step, history entry and setup field — the ten record types that were still first-tab-only. With this batch every drawer tab in the planner is drawn.

Four of these ten are about the planner, not the wedding

Batch 26 · drawer tabs · Covenant

Four records where the prose is the point

Vision section, prayer entry, counseling session and rhythm — from 13a, 13b, 13c and 13d. These are the Class B records, and they break two habits the other twenty do not.

Prose is the record, not a note about it

Everywhere else a text field is a note beside the data. Here the wording is the data, so it renders in Cormorant inside the 360px drawer — because it will print in Cormorant, and a keepsake edited in the wrong face gets written in the wrong voice.

Print is a tab here and nowhere else

For every other record printing is a by-product. For a Covenant record the printed page is the deliverable, so the drawer shows the page proof and logs each print — an edited keepsake means the paper copy in the drawer at home is now wrong.

Status is derived from the prose

A prayer is answered when an answer exists — there is no tick. A session is complete when its homework rows are, not when someone says so. Both refuse the separate flag that could disagree with the thing it describes.

The one record that cannot be shared

Prayer is greyed out in the share-packet picker rather than hidden, so you can see it was withheld on purpose. Privacy belongs to the record type, which is why that tab shows facts instead of toggles.

Batch 22 · drawer tabs · Money

Two money records, eight tabs

Every drawer in the planner shows its tab strip, but until now only the first tab was drawn. This batch works through them a category at a time, starting with Money — the budget line item from 4a and the contract from 10c. Every figure is read from those two screens, not invented.

Money records tab by who is owed and when

A budget line splits into the line, its category, its payment and its history. Not into “details / more details” — each tab answers a question someone actually asks: what does it cost, what allowance does it eat, who is paying it, and who changed it.

The variance lives on the category

A line item can go over estimate; only the category can be over budget. So the $85 variance is stated on the Line item tab and carried on the Category tab, and the History tab shows that both moved in one edit.

A missing thing is a stated thing

The line item has no contract behind its $265. The contract has no payment schedule and two documents outstanding. All three are drawn as warnings inside the tab that owns them, rather than left for a reconciliation later.

Only a contract total is authoritative

Editing it re-derives the Budget row, the outstanding balance and the Dashboard percentage — so that one edit confirms first. A line-item actual does not: it is already the lowest level.
