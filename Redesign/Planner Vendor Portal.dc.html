Vendor portal · a separate product

What the caterer sees

The vendor view is not the planner with things switched off. It is four tabs, its own desaturated chrome, no login, and a scope contract that decides what can be reached rather than what is displayed. It gets its own document because it is its own feature — a different audience, a different trust model, and the only surface in the system a stranger will ever open.

One rule holds the whole thing up. Counts without names. The kitchen needs to know three guests have a nut allergy; it does not need to know which three. Every other decision here follows from that one.

Your brief
The landing tab. Counts, their slice of the run sheet, what they owe, and two numbers — deliberately not the full contact list. Everything a caterer needs to work from, with no guest named anywhere on the page.
Build notes — for implementation
Purpose: One screen a vendor can work from without asking a question. It replaces the email thread.
Counts, never names: 142 covers, 9 vegetarian, 3 nut allergies. The kitchen needs the numbers; it does not need to know which guests. This is the rule the whole portal rests on.
Derived times: Their obligations are read live from the couple’s Wedding Day Timeline. If the couple moves dinner, this page moves with it — no re-send, no stale PDF.
You owe us: The only role view with a section about what the viewer owes. Dated, reasoned, and consequence-stated: the venue will not release keys without the certificate.
Two numbers, not forty-seven: A vendor gets the planner and the venue. Other vendors’ numbers are not theirs to have.

Their window on a clock, and only their window. Two dependencies are shown because they cut both ways — the venue waits on their canapés, the band works around their cake cut.
Build notes — for implementation
Their rows only: Five rows, all theirs. The other five vendors’ bars are not drawn. A vendor seeing the whole day sees other people’s business.

▸Paperwork and upload2 screens
V3
Your paperwork
Their contract, their instalments, their invoices. The closing line is the point: the couple’s budget and other vendors’ pricing are not hidden behind a permission, they are absent from the model.
Build notes — for implementation
Scope: One contract, its child instalments, and the invoices raised against them. Nothing belonging to another vendor is reachable from here, by any route.
Instalments are derived: Child records of the contract per §13, so the vendor sees the same schedule the couple sees, and neither party can type a figure the other does not have.
Clause surfacing: The one clause that governs a deadline is lifted into a row — final headcount locks 1 November — rather than being left inside a PDF nobody reopens.
Raise an invoice: The only write action a vendor has on money. It creates a draft the couple must accept; it does not post to their budget directly.

▸Mobile, and the end of access1 screen
V5
Mobile, and the expired link
A caterer reads this on a phone in a kitchen. Three things stack: next obligation, what they owe, and one-tap calling. And the state every share eventually reaches — expired, stated without blame.
Build notes — for implementation
Mobile is the primary case: Vendors are rarely at a desk. The phone layout leads with the next obligation and the overdue item, then calling. Nothing else survives the fold.
Tabs scroll, never collapse: Four tabs fit horizontally with a scroll. A vendor portal with a hamburger is a vendor portal nobody explores.
Expired is not an error: Access ends four days after the wedding by design. The screen states what still belongs to them and offers a route back, without implying they did something wrong.
No login: A vendor holds a link, not an account. The portal never asks a caterer to create a password to read their own call time.

Scope and lifecycle

What can be reached, and how someone gets in and out. These two screens are the ones an implementer needs; the four tabs above are what they produce.

▸Scope and access2 screens
V6
The scope contract
Fifteen rows deciding what a vendor can ever see, each with the reason beside it. The reasons matter more than the marks — they are what lets someone extend this table correctly in a year.
Build notes — for implementation
This is the security model: Not a settings screen. Every ✕ is absent from the query, not filtered out of a response. A vendor endpoint that could return a guest name is a bug, not a misconfiguration.
“counts” is its own value: Neither yes nor no. Dietary requirements pass through as aggregates — 3 nut, 9 vegetarian — and the identities never leave the couple’s side.
Two numbers, not a directory: Day-of contact resolves to the planner and the venue. Other vendors are reachable through the planner, deliberately.
Reasons are load-bearing: When someone adds a sixteenth row they need the principle, not the precedent. Every row carries why.

V7
Access lifecycle
Five steps from packet to expiry, and an honest account of what revoking actually achieves. No account creation anywhere in the flow — a caterer should not need a password to read their own call time.
Build notes — for implementation
No content picker: The planner chooses the vendor; the scope contract chooses the contents. Removing the choice removes the mistake.
Token in a link: No login. Access is a URL with an expiry, which is the same trust model as a calendar invite.
Writes are attributed: Uploads and schedule responses land as notes on the vendor record, so the couple’s history shows who did what.
Expiry is automatic: Four days after the wedding. A permanent vendor share is not offered.
Honest revocation: Stated in the dialog and here: it stops the link, it does not recall a PDF, and it does not delete what they uploaded.
How a vendor gets in, and out
1
Planner builds the packet
From Share Packets. Picks the vendor, the portal decides the contents from the scope contract — there is no content picker.
2
Link is sent
A URL with an embedded token. No account, no password. The vendor is a caterer, not a user we are trying to acquire.
3
Vendor opens it
Provenance banner names who shared it and when access ends. First open is logged and surfaces in the couple’s Share Packets · Activity view.
4
Vendor works from it
Reads their brief, accepts the schedule, uploads what they owe. Every write is attributed and lands as a note on their vendor record.
5
Access expires
Four days after the wedding, automatically. Downloaded files stay theirs; the live view closes.
What revocation can and cannot do
Stops the live linkimmediately
Ends further downloadsimmediately
Removes them from activityno · the log is kept
Recalls a downloaded PDFno · impossible
Deletes what they uploadedno · it is the couple’s now
Said plainly in the revoke dialog too. A planner who believes revocation recalls a PDF will make a worse decision than one who knows it does not.
